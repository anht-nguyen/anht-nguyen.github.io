Action observation engages movement-related brain activity without requiring a person to complete the movement. I investigate how these responses depend on the action being shown and whether it is demonstrated by a human or a robot.

## Research questions

- How do sensorimotor EEG rhythms change during observation of human and humanoid robot actions?
- Which functional-connectivity measures distinguish observation conditions and observed actions?
- How can EEG measures inform individualized rehabilitation after stroke?

## Work to date

My first-author BioRob 2024 paper examined EEG responses in three healthy adults watching human and humanoid robot demonstrations. The pilot showed substantial variation between individuals, motivating closer study of individual neural responses.

In a subsequent exploratory study with five healthy adults, we compared EEG functional-connectivity measures and machine learning models for decoding observation conditions and action types. This work is available as a preprint.

My 2026 scoping review with Michelle J. Johnson synthesized 22 studies of EEG during upper-limb action observation after stroke. It examines candidate neural markers, relationships with motor outcomes, and differences in study protocols that complicate comparison across studies.

## Ongoing direction

My doctoral research investigates how action-observation EEG measures relate to upper-limb motor function after stroke and how they might support individualized feedback. The pilot studies establish feasibility; larger studies are needed to evaluate reliability and clinical utility.
