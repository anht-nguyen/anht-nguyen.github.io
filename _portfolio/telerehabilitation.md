Rehabilitation technology must work for the people who use it. My collaborative work examines patient preferences and clinicians’ experiences with video communication and social robot-augmented telepresence.

## Patient preferences

Our 2025 study in *IEEE Transactions on Neural Systems and Rehabilitation Engineering* compared in-person, conventional video, and robot-augmented video interactions in 42 participants. In-person interaction was preferred overall, while 71% of participants preferred the robot-augmented condition over conventional video. Preferences varied with age and motor and cognitive function.

I contributed equally with Michael J. Sobrepera and served as corresponding author. The study evaluated preferences in simulated care, rather than clinical treatment efficacy.

## Clinical deployment & therapist perspectives

A 2024 *Robotica* study examined Flo, a social robot-augmented telepresence system, across 23 sessions with 11 patients in an elder care clinic. A separate survey of 387 rehabilitation therapists examined telecommunication use before and during COVID-19, alongside intended use after the pandemic.

Together, these studies help connect interface design with patient needs, therapist workflows, and the practical limits of remote rehabilitation.
